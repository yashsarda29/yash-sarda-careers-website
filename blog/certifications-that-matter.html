Which certifications actually matter?

It's easy to collect certificates. It's harder to collect ones a recruiter actually cares about. Here's how to tell the difference.

Yash SardaMBA, Great Lakes Chennai · 5+ years in industry

Scroll through student LinkedIn profiles and you'll see long lists of certificates: online courses, workshops, webinars, quizzes. Recruiters see the same lists all day, and most of them barely register.

A few certifications genuinely help. The trick is knowing which ones, and spending your time on those.

The issueThe certificate trap

A certificate that anyone can get by watching videos for an afternoon doesn't separate you from anyone. Ten of those don't add up to one strong signal. Worse, a long list can bury the one thing on your profile that does matter.

The testThe three-question filter

Before you start any certification, ask:

  1. Does my target role ask for it? Look at ten job descriptions for the role. If a certification or the skill behind it keeps appearing, it's worth considering. If it never appears, it probably isn't.
  2. Does it require real proof? Certifications with a proctored exam, a graded project or hands-on work carry more weight than attendance certificates.
  3. Is it recognised? Is it from the company that makes the tool, a well-known university or platform, or a respected industry body?

If the answer to all three is yes, it's probably worth your time. If it's no to all three, skip it.

The landscapeThree kinds of certification

1. Platform and tool certifications

Issued by the company behind a tool, such as cloud platforms, analytics tools or marketing platforms. Useful when a role uses that exact tool, because they show you can work with it from day one.

2. Course certificates from universities and learning platforms

Courses on NPTEL, Coursera and similar platforms are valuable for learning in a structured way. The certificate itself is a modest signal; what you build with the knowledge is the real proof.

3. Professional qualifications

Longer, exam-based qualifications in fields like finance, accounting or project management. They're a serious commitment, so only take one on if it clearly fits your career plan.

A project you built with a skill beats a certificate that says you studied it.

On paperHow to show them

  • On your CV, list only the two or three most relevant ones, with the issuer and year.
  • Pair each with something you did using it: "Google Analytics certified; used it to track our fest website's traffic."
  • On LinkedIn, add them in the Licences & certifications section with the verification link.

NextWhat to do next

Look at your current list and apply the filter. Keep the strongest few, and turn one into a small project.
