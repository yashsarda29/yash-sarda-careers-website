How to land an internship with no experience.

Every internship asks for experience, and you need an internship to get experience. Here's how to break that loop in a few focused weeks.

Yash SardaMBA, Great Lakes Chennai · 5+ years in industry

Most first internships aren't won by the student with the most experience. They go to the student who made it easy for someone to say yes: a clear interest, a small piece of proof, and a direct, polite message to the right person.

Here's a plan you can start this week.

Step 1You have more experience than you think

"Experience" doesn't only mean a previous internship. Recruiters for entry-level roles also count:

  • Academic and personal projects
  • Club, fest or society responsibilities (organising, sponsorship, social media)
  • Competitions, hackathons and case challenges
  • Freelance work, tutoring or helping in a family business

Write each one down with what you did and what came out of it. That's your raw material.

Step 2Build one piece of proof

If your list feels thin, spend one or two weekends on a small project related to the internship you want. Want a marketing internship? Analyse three brands' Instagram strategies and write up what you'd change. Want a data internship? Clean and analyse a public dataset and publish the result.

Why this worksA small, finished project shows initiative and skill at the same time, and it gives you something specific to talk about in every message and interview.

Step 3Make a target list

Don't just apply on big job portals, where hundreds of people apply for every post. Build a list of about 30 organisations:

  • 10 well-known companies in your field
  • 10 startups and smaller firms, which often hire interns without formal postings
  • 10 alumni or seniors' workplaces, where you have a warm connection

For each one, find a real person: a team lead, founder, or alumnus. LinkedIn and your college's alumni network are the best places to look.

Step 4Reach out directly

Keep your message short, specific and easy to reply to:

"Hi Priya, I'm a second-year BBA student at XYZ and I'm interested in marketing at your company. I recently analysed three D2C brands' Instagram strategies (link). Are you taking interns this summer? I'd be happy to share my CV."

It names the person, shows genuine interest, offers proof, and asks one simple question. Avoid long life stories and "please give me any opportunity".

Step 5The weekly plan

  1. Week 1: list your experience, update your CV and LinkedIn, and pick your project.
  2. Week 2: finish the project and build your target list of 30.
  3. Week 3: send 10 personalised messages and apply to 5 postings.
  4. Week 4: send 10 more, follow up on week 3, and prepare for interviews.

Track everything in a simple spreadsheet: name, company, date contacted, reply, next step.

Step 6Follow up, politely

Most replies come after a follow-up, not the first message. Wait about a week, then send one short, friendly note. If there's still no answer, move on without taking it personally. People are busy.
