CAT, GMAT or GRE? Choosing the right exam.

Students often pick the exam first and the programme later. Do it the other way round and the choice usually becomes obvious.

Yash SardaMBA, Great Lakes Chennai · 5+ years in industry

"Should I write CAT or GMAT?" is a question I hear often, and it's usually asked too early. The right exam depends almost entirely on which programmes you want to apply to. So start there.

NoteExam formats, fees and accepted scores change. Always confirm the latest details on each exam's and each programme's official website before you plan.

Step 1Start with programmes, not exams

Make a shortlist of 8 to 10 programmes you'd genuinely be happy to attend. Mix ambitious and realistic choices. For each, note the exam they accept, the work experience they expect, and their application deadlines.

Once that list exists, look at which exam covers most of it. That's usually your answer.

IndiaCAT

  • The main entrance exam for the IIMs and many other Indian business schools.
  • Held once a year, usually towards the end of the year, with results used for the following year's admissions.
  • Known for a demanding time-pressured format, especially in quantitative and logical sections.

If your shortlist is mostly Indian two-year programmes, CAT is likely your main exam. Some Indian schools also run or accept their own tests, so check each one.

Abroad, and some Indian schoolsGMAT and GRE

  • Accepted by a large number of business schools around the world.
  • Also accepted by some Indian programmes, particularly one-year and executive-style MBAs.
  • Can be taken at many points during the year, and you can retake them; scores typically stay valid for several years.

The GRE is also used for many master's programmes outside business, which makes it useful if you're still deciding between an MBA and a specialised master's degree.

The choiceHow to decide

Mostly Indian two-year MBAsFocus on CAT (plus any school-specific tests on your list).
Mostly programmes abroadGMAT or GRE. Check which your target schools prefer, if any.

Still torn? Take a free practice test for each and see which format suits you. Your comfort with the format matters, because you'll be practising it for months.

The best exam is the one your target programmes accept and you can score well on.

PlanPlan your timeline backwards

  1. Write down the application deadlines for your shortlist.
  2. Plan to have your final score at least a month or two before the first deadline.
  3. Count back three to six months of preparation, depending on your starting point.
  4. Leave room for a retake if your exam allows it.
