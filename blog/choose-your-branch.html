CS or ECE? Choose a specialisation you won't regret.

Stop comparing subjects. Compare what an ordinary Tuesday looks like three years from now.

Yash SardaMBA, Great Lakes Chennai · 5+ years in industry

CS or ECE. Mechanical or something "safer". Finance or Marketing. Whatever the pair, students usually decide in one of three ways: follow the crowd, follow the highest placement package they've heard about, or follow whichever subject they scored best in last semester.

None of those is terrible, but none of them asks the most important question: what will you actually be doing every day? Here's a four-question framework that does.

Question 01Which kind of work do you enjoy, not which subject?

Subjects are how a field is taught. Jobs are how it's practised, and the two can feel very different. Think about the activities you lose track of time doing:

  • Building and debugging until something finally works?
  • Working with hardware, circuits and physical systems?
  • Finding patterns in numbers and making a call on them?
  • Persuading people, understanding customers, telling a story?

Your answer points more reliably to a specialisation than your marks do.

Question 02Where does each path lead in three years?

For each option, list three or four real job titles people hold a few years after graduating. Then find people in those roles, on LinkedIn or among your seniors, and ask them what a normal week looks like.

Try thisMessage three people on LinkedIn: "I'm a student deciding between X and Y. Could I ask you two quick questions about your role?" Most people are kinder than you expect, and ten minutes of their time beats ten hours of reading rankings.

Question 03How easy is it to switch later?

Some choices are more reversible than they look. Many ECE graduates move into software roles, for example, and many engineers move into business roles through an MBA. Other moves are harder, like going from a non-technical background into core hardware design.

If you're torn, lean towards the option that keeps more doors open, while being honest about whether you'd enjoy it.

Question 04What does your evidence say?

Feelings change; evidence is more stable. Look at what you've actually done: the projects you finished without being asked, the electives you chose, the clubs you stayed in. Those choices reveal your interests better than any aptitude quiz.

The best specialisation is the one you'll still be curious about on a bad day.

Run a one-week test

Still unsure? Give each option one small, real test before you commit:

  1. Pick a beginner project for each option, one you can finish in a weekend.
  2. Notice which one you wanted to keep working on after it was done.
  3. Talk to one senior in each field about what surprised them.

A week of trying beats a month of thinking.

Three myths to drop

  • "Pick the one with the highest package." Average packages hide huge variation, and you'll be competing with people who love the field.
  • "This choice decides my whole life." It shapes your first few years, not your entire career. People switch more often than you think.
  • "I'll figure out if I like it once I start." You can find out much sooner, and much more cheaply, with the test above.

What to do next

Write down your options, run the four questions for each, and try the one-week test.
