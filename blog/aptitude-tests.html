Cracking aptitude tests: what to practise, and in what order.

Aptitude tests are usually the first filter in campus placements. They're very learnable, if you practise the right things in the right order.

Yash SardaMBA, Great Lakes Chennai · 5+ years in industry

Many companies use an online aptitude test to shortlist from hundreds or thousands of applicants. Students who are strong in their subjects still get filtered out here, not because the questions are hard, but because they're unprepared for the speed.

The formatWhat the tests usually cover

Formats vary by company, so always check what you can find about yours, but most tests include some mix of:

  • Quantitative: percentages, ratios, averages, profit and loss, time and work, speed and distance, and data interpretation from tables and charts.
  • Logical reasoning: series, coding-decoding, arrangements, syllogisms, blood relations and puzzles.
  • Verbal: reading comprehension, grammar, sentence correction and vocabulary in context.

Some tech roles add a coding or technical section too.

The planA 4-week practice order

  1. Week 1, foundations: percentages, ratios and averages. They show up inside almost every other quant topic, so start here.
  2. Week 2, core quant: time and work, speed and distance, profit and loss, and data interpretation.
  3. Week 3, reasoning and verbal: one reasoning topic and one reading comprehension set every day.
  4. Week 4, full mocks: two or three timed mock tests, each followed by a careful review.
Daily habit45 to 60 minutes a day beats a six-hour cram on Sunday. Short, regular practice is what builds speed.

On the dayTest-day tactics

  • Read the rules first: is there negative marking? Sectional time limits? That decides how much you should guess.
  • Use the two-pass method: answer everything you can quickly, mark the rest, then come back.
  • Set a skip rule: if a question isn't going anywhere after about a minute, move on.
  • Protect accuracy: a wrong answer with negative marking costs more than a skipped one.
Speed comes from recognising question types instantly, and that only comes from volume.

The secretKeep an error log

After every practice set, write down each question you got wrong and why: a concept gap, a calculation slip, misreading the question, or running out of time. Review the log every few days. You'll quickly see the two or three topics costing you most marks, and fixing those is the fastest way to improve.
