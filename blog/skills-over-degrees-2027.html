Skills over degrees: what employers want in 2027.

Your degree still opens the door. But the latest hiring reports show the first screen is now about what you can do, and what you can show.

Yash SardaMBA, Great Lakes Chennai · 5+ years in industry

If you are heading into placements in 2027, the rules have quietly changed. Employers still hire freshers, and plenty of them. What has changed is how they decide who gets through.

The shiftWhat the latest reports say

Three reports from 2026 point the same way:

  • Skills and proof of work over degree filters. TeamLease EdTech's Career Outlook for the first half of 2026 found 73% of employers planned to hire freshers, but hiring is moving from "degree and resume filters" to skills, proof of work and behaviour. Source
  • AI is changing the hiring process. Deloitte's Campus Workforce Trends 2026 report says 85% of Indian companies feel AI is reshaping how they hire graduates. Source
  • AI and data roles are growing fastest. Naukri's JobSpeak for August 2026 recorded 31% more AI and machine-learning hiring and 15% more fresher hiring than a year earlier. Source
In shortEmployers want to see that you can apply what you studied. A good degree with nothing to show is now weaker than a decent degree with real proof.

In demandThe skills employers named

The TeamLease report lists AI fluency, cybersecurity awareness, cloud and DevOps, data skills, digital marketing analytics and workplace adaptability. You don't need all of them. You need one or two that fit the role you want, with something to prove them.

What to doFive things to do before 2027 placements

  1. Finish one real project. Pick a problem, build or analyse something, and write up what happened. One finished project beats five half-done courses.
  2. Get an internship or live work, even a short one. Employers count internships, apprenticeships, hackathons and competitions as proof of work.
  3. Learn to use AI tools well. Use them to research, draft and practise, and be ready to explain how you used them and where you checked their work.
  4. Make your CV pass the first screen. Software often sorts CVs before a person reads them. Use a simple layout, clear section headings and the words the job description uses.
  5. Show your work in one place. A LinkedIn profile, GitHub or a simple portfolio link on your CV lets a recruiter check your claims in one click.
