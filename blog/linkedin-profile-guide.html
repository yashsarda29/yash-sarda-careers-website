Your LinkedIn profile in 30 minutes: what recruiters actually read.

Recruiters don't read your whole profile. They read four things, in a few seconds. Get those right and the rest matters much less.

Yash SardaMBA, Great Lakes Chennai · 5+ years in industry

Many students set up LinkedIn in first year, add a college name and a few skills, and never touch it again. Then placement season arrives and they wonder why no recruiter ever reaches out.

The good news: you don't need hundreds of connections or daily posts. You need a profile that answers one question fast: what does this person do, and are they relevant to my role?

The basicsWhat recruiters see first

When your profile shows up in a search, a recruiter sees your photo, name, headline and location. If those don't earn a click, nothing else on your profile gets read. Once they click, they scan the About section and the top of Experience.

  • Photo: a clear, well-lit head-and-shoulders shot, facing the camera, with a plain background. No group photos or sunglasses.
  • Banner: optional, but a simple one related to your field looks better than the default.
  • Location: set it to where you want to work, as recruiters often filter by city.

Most importantYour headline

By default, LinkedIn shows "Student at XYZ College". That tells a recruiter nothing. Your headline is searchable, so use it to say what you do and what you're aiming for.

BeforeStudent at XYZ College of Engineering
AfterFinal-year B.Tech (ECE) · Data analysis, SQL, Power BI · Looking for analyst roles

A simple formula: who you are · 2 to 3 key skills · what you're looking for.

Your storyYour About section

Write it in the first person, in short paragraphs. Three parts work well:

  1. What you do and care about, in one or two lines.
  2. Proof: your best two or three achievements, with results where you can.
  3. What you're looking for, and how to reach you.

Keep it under 150 words. The first two lines show before "see more", so make them count.

ProofExperience and projects

Treat this like your CV: results, not duties. Add internships, part-time work, club roles and freelance work. Then use the Projects section for academic and personal projects, with a link to GitHub, a portfolio or a short write-up.

A project with a link beats a skill with an endorsement.

SearchSkills and keywords

Recruiters search LinkedIn the way you search Google: by keywords. Look at three job descriptions for your target role, note the skills they repeat, and make sure the ones you genuinely have appear in your headline, About section, experience and skills list.

Pin your top three skills. Remove irrelevant ones that dilute your profile.

Do it nowThe 30-minute checklist

  1. Upload a clear profile photo (5 min).
  2. Rewrite your headline with the formula above (5 min).
  3. Write a three-part About section (10 min).
  4. Add one project with a link (5 min).
  5. Update your skills using keywords from real job descriptions (5 min).

Then make sure your profile tells the same story as your CV. If you want a sharper version, the LinkedIn review session goes through your profile line by line, and the CV and LinkedIn rebuild aligns both to the roles you're targeting.
